### 2.4 The bridge FIT itself defines

**ISM Matematický seminář** exists "to revise and fill in missing secondary-school
mathematics needed for further study… especially what is a necessary prerequisite for
IMA1". Its exercise outline:

1. Manipulating algebraic expressions
2. Equations and inequalities I — linear, quadratic, rational, **with absolute value**
3. Function concept, graph, properties, **shifts and transformations of graphs**; **linear and quadratic functions**
4. **Power**, linear-fractional, **exponential and logarithmic** functions; **finding the inverse function**
5. **Trigonometric functions** (properties and transformations), goniometry; composition of functions
6–7. **Complex numbers**
8. Equations and inequalities II — **exponential and logarithmic**
9. Equations and inequalities III — irrational, **trigonometric**, mixed
10. Analytic geometry (line and plane, positions, distances)
11–12. Conic sections

Bold items are on Jonas's 2nd-year syllabus. **This is the single most useful finding of the
research:** the material FIT considers the mathematical entry requirement for its analysis
course is, to a large extent, exactly what he is being taught this year. Lemma's FIT
roadmap uses the ISM outline as its definition of "high-school mathematics FIT expects",
with the source attached.

The same content appears in the paid preparatory course **"FIT Start: Matematika bez
obav"** (<https://www.fit.vut.cz/applicants/pripravny-kurz-matematika/.cs>): algebraic
expressions, equations and inequalities; functions; goniometry; complex numbers; analytic
geometry and conics. The 2026 run: 17 Sep – 10 Dec 2026, Thursdays 16:00–17:50, 40 places,
1 990 Kč, aimed at final-year students. Passing its final test is a route to preferential
admission and can be recognised as ISM (2 credits).

### 2.5 Admission — rules for academic year 2027/2028

Source: **Směrnice č. 2/2026**, approved by the FIT academic senate on 22 Sep 2026
(<https://www.fit.vut.cz/fit/info/smernice/2026/sm2026-2.pdf>), and the applicants page
<https://www.fit.vut.cz/applicants/application/.cs>.

> These rules apply to applicants starting in **2027/2028**. Jonas applies for
> **2029/2030**. They are recorded as a dated snapshot, not as what he will face.

- Basic condition: secondary education with maturita.
- **No faculty entrance exam.** Applicants are ranked by their best overall percentile in
  the SCIO National Comparative Exams (NSZ) — **OSP**, **MAT** or **VŠP** — across all NSZ
  dates in the preceding academic year.
- Expected initial admission threshold: **percentile 75**; once set it cannot be raised,
  it may be lowered. (Art. 2 separately names percentile 10 as the formal minimum.)
- At most **800** applicants admitted. Applicants page: 1 621 applicants the previous year,
  84 % acceptance rate.
- Application window 1 Nov 2026 – 31 Mar 2027. One NSZ sitting is free for applicants who
  applied and paid on time (24 Apr 2027); substitute date 22 May 2027.
- **Preferential admission** (art. 7) — no NSZ needed — if at least one holds. The complete
  list has 23 items (a–w); those realistically relevant to a Czech secondary student:
  - (b) successful solver of the **regional round of the Mathematical Olympiad**, category A, B, C or **P**
  - (c) successful solver of the regional round of the Physics Olympiad, category A–D
  - (d) 1st–3rd place, regional round of the Logical Olympiad (Mensa), category C
  - (e) 1st–3rd place at the regional showcase of **SOČ**; (j) SOČ topic presented under a FIT/FEKT VUT supervisor
  - (k) **Matematický klokan**, category Student: successful solver of the national round, or percentile ≥ 80
  - (l) **Bobřík informatiky**, category Senior: places 1–20
  - (p) final round of the national cybersecurity competition with ≥ 20 % of points
  - (q) 1st–3rd place in a technical competition (co-)organised by FIT, FEKT or FSI VUT
  - (t) **maturita in mathematics with percentile ≥ 80**
  - (u) Matematika+ (the selective maths exam) with percentile ≥ 60
  - (v) Mensa IQ test, percentile ≥ 90
  - (w) successfully completed FIT's **preparatory mathematics course for maturita students**
  - Items (b)–(q) and (w) must not be older than 4 years; (r)–(v) have no time limit.
- The English-language bachelor's programme is not opened in 2027/2028.

Note a discrepancy handled conservatively: the applicants web page summarises one route as
"successful solver of a *school* olympiad"; the binding directive says **regional round**.
Lemma follows the directive.

## 3. Learning science

**[literature]** unless marked. Each finding is followed by the design decision it drives;
the full mechanics are in `learning-model.md`.

| Finding | Source | Decision in Lemma |
|---|---|---|
| Practice testing and distributed practice are the two study techniques with the strongest evidence; interleaving is promising; rereading and highlighting are weak. | Dunlosky, Rawson, Marsh, Nathan & Willingham (2013), *Psychological Science in the Public Interest* 14(1) | Lessons are short and end in retrieval; there is no "notes" mode. |
| Retrieving information strengthens it more than restudying. | Roediger & Karpicke (2006), *Psychological Science* 17(3) | Every review is a problem to solve, never a card to reread. |
| Spacing reviews beats massing them; the optimal gap grows with the retention interval. | Cepeda, Pashler, Vul, Wixted & Rohrer (2006), *Psychological Bulletin* 132(3) | Per-skill expanding review schedule. |
| Mixing problem types forces the learner to *choose* a method; blocked practice hides that step. Large delayed-test gains in school maths. | Rohrer & Taylor (2007), *Instructional Science* 35; Rohrer, Dedrick & Stershic (2015), *J. Educational Psychology* 107(3) | Reviews are interleaved and the topic label is hidden. "Mixed" successes are a separate kind of evidence. |
| Conditions that slow apparent learning (spacing, interleaving, testing) improve long-term retention. | Bjork (1994); Bjork & Bjork (2011) | Target success rate ~70–85 %, not 100 %. The UI never treats a wrong answer as a failure state. |
| Novices learn more from studying worked examples than from unguided solving; the advantage reverses with expertise. | Sweller & Cooper (1985), *Cognition and Instruction* 2(1); Kalyuga et al. (2003), *Educational Psychologist* 38(1) | New concepts start with worked examples; support fades as ability rises. |
| Fading steps from worked examples to independent solving outperforms an abrupt switch. | Renkl & Atkinson (2003), *Educational Psychologist* 38(1) | Lesson step types: worked → completion → independent. |
| Explaining steps to oneself improves understanding and transfer. | Chi, Bassok, Lewis, Reimann & Glaser (1989), *Cognitive Science* 13 | "Why is this step allowed?" prompts; explanation-type questions. |
| Attempting a problem before instruction can improve conceptual understanding. | Kapur (2008), *Cognition and Instruction* 26(3) | Lessons open with a *predict* step before any explanation. |
| Studying incorrect worked examples improves conceptual understanding and error detection. | Booth, Lange, Koedinger & Newton (2013), *Learning and Instruction* 25; Durkin & Rittle-Johnson (2012), *Learning and Instruction* 22(3) | "Find the mistake" is a first-class problem type and the core of the Error Lab. |
| Errors made with high confidence are the most likely to be corrected after feedback. | Butterfield & Metcalfe (2001), *JEP: LMC* 27(6) | Optional confidence rating before submitting; confident errors are surfaced, not buried. |
| Procedural errors are often systematic "bugs", diagnosable from the wrong answer. | Brown & Burton (1978), *Cognitive Science* 2 | Problems carry misconception signatures: specific wrong answers mapped to specific causes. |
| Errors on written maths tasks fall into stages: reading, comprehension, transformation, process, encoding — plus carelessness. | Newman (1977); Radatz (1979), *JRME* 10(3) | The error taxonomy has three families (slip / procedure / concept) rather than one flat list. |
| Mastery learning: progress on demonstrated mastery, with corrective feedback. | Bloom (1968; 1984, *Educational Researcher* 13(6)) | Prerequisite graph gates new material; levels need evidence. |
| Expertise comes from practice aimed at specific weaknesses with immediate feedback. | Ericsson, Krampe & Tesch-Römer (1993), *Psychological Review* 100(3) | Error Lab drills target the learner's own most frequent error type. |
| Knowledge tracing: estimate latent skill from a sequence of answers, with explicit *slip* and *guess*. | Corbett & Anderson (1995), *UMUAI* 4 | Wrong answers on a skill the model considers strong are classified as probable slips. |
| An Elo-style rating is a simple, robust alternative to knowledge tracing for adaptive practice. | Pelánek (2016), *Computers & Education* 98 | Ability per skill is an Elo-like estimate against authored item difficulty. |
| FSRS: a memory model (difficulty, stability, retrievability) for scheduling reviews. | Ye, Su & Cao (2022), KDD; open-spaced-repetition project | Review scheduling uses `ts-fsrs` behind an interface. |
| In hierarchical subjects, practising an advanced topic implicitly reviews its components; credit should flow down an "encompassing" graph. | Skycak, "Fractional Implicit Repetition" **[fetched]** <https://justinmath.com/individualized-spaced-repetition-in-hierarchical-knowledge-structures/> | Concepts declare `encompasses` edges; a simplified credit flow reduces redundant reviews. |
| Autonomy, competence and relatedness sustain intrinsic motivation; expected external rewards can undermine it. | Ryan & Deci (2000), *American Psychologist* 55(1); Lepper, Greene & Nisbett (1973) | No XP, no currency. Progress is shown as evidence of competence; every recommendation can be overridden. |
| Feedback about the task and the process helps; feedback about the person does not. | Hattie & Timperley (2007), *Review of Educational Research* 77(1) | Feedback names the step and the cause, never the learner. |

Honest limits: these are group-level findings from classrooms and labs. The specific
thresholds in Lemma (how many mixed successes make "proficient", how much a hint costs) are
engineering choices informed by this literature, not values taken from it. They are
constants in one file and are meant to be tuned against Jonas's own data.

### 4.1 Mathematical visualisation

**[fetched]** versions from the npm registry, 2026-10-07.

| Option | State | Assessment |
|---|---|---|
| **Mafs** 0.21.0 (MIT) | Last published 2025-03-30; React ≥ 18 | The nicest API for interactive maths in React, but 18 months without a release. A risk for a product meant to live for years. |
| **JSXGraph** 1.14.0 (MIT/LGPL) | Active (published 2026-10-05) | Very capable for geometric constructions; imperative API with its own rendering, harder to theme. Reserved for a future construction tool. |
| **function-plot** 1.25.4 (MIT) | Active | Fine for static function plots; not designed for draggable, lesson-embedded figures. |
| Desmos API, GeoGebra embed | Proprietary / external service | Rejected: violates "no external SaaS", cannot be themed, heavy. |
| **In-house SVG figure kit** | — | **Chosen.** |

Reasoning: the same figure has to appear in three places — inside a lesson (interactive),
inside a problem (static, generated from data) and in the Lab (free-form). A small SVG kit
driven by a serialisable *figure spec* serves all three, follows the theme through CSS
variables, and removes the largest third-party risk. Cost: roughly a thousand lines we own.

**KaTeX** 0.19 (MIT) renders mathematics; it is fast, synchronous and fully self-hosted.

### 4.2 Answer checking

Options considered: SymPy (Python — would force a second language and service), `mathjs`
15.2 (large, general), `@cortex-js/compute-engine` 0.149 (pre-1.0, API still moving), or an
in-house parser.

**Chosen: in-house expression parser and evaluator.** Equivalence of algebraic answers is
decided by evaluating both expressions at pseudo-random points — the approach used for
decades by WeBWorK — which needs only a parser and an evaluator. Owning the parser is what
makes Czech notation possible: decimal comma, `tg`/`cotg`, `log` meaning base 10, intervals
`⟨a; b)`, `K = {…}`, `R \ {2}`. No third-party library handles these, and they are exactly
what school tests and the maturita use (§2.7).

### 4.3 Spaced repetition

`ts-fsrs` 5.4.2 (MIT, published 2026-10-03). **[fetched]** Verified locally:
`fsrs(generatorParameters(...))`, `createEmptyCard`, `next(card, now, rating)`,
`get_retrievability`. FSRS parameters were fitted on flashcard recall, not on procedural
maths skills, so absolute intervals are a starting point. The scheduler sits behind an
interface for that reason.

### 4.4 GitHub and Forgejo

**[fetched]** GitHub REST documentation and live probes.

- `GET /users/{username}/events/public` works **without authentication**: 60 requests/hour
  per IP, `ETag` support (a `304` does not consume quota), `X-Poll-Interval: 60`. At most
  300 events from the **past 30 days**; latency 30 s – 6 h. Push events no longer list
  commits (payload keys: `before`, `head`, `push_id`, `ref`, `repository_id`), so the
  honest unit is "events", not "commits".
- The contribution calendar (`contributionsCollection.contributionCalendar`) exists only in
  the **GraphQL API, which rejects unauthenticated requests**. A token with no scopes is
  enough for public data.
- The HTML fragment at `github.com/users/<name>/contributions` is undocumented; not used.
- **Forgejo / Gitea**: `GET /api/v1/users/{username}/heatmap` returns
  `[{timestamp, contributions}]` publicly (verified against codeberg.org and its Swagger
  definition). Relevant because ModulBit runs on Forgejo.

Decision: username only → REST events (last 30 days, labelled as such); optional token →
full-year calendar via GraphQL; optional Forgejo base URL + username → heatmap. Everything
is cached in the database and shown stale-with-a-timestamp when the network fails.

### 4.6 Self-hosting

- **SQLite in WAL mode** on a named volume. One user, one writer: a database server would
  add a container, credentials and a dump procedure for no benefit. Backups use SQLite's
  online backup API, producing a consistent single file while the app runs.
- **One container.** The API serves the built frontend; periodic jobs run in-process.
- **Behind a proxy / Cloudflare Tunnel**: TLS terminates upstream, so cookies get the
  `Secure` flag from configuration, and the client address is taken from forwarding
  headers only when `TRUST_PROXY=1`. Streaming tutor responses use server-sent events over
  plain HTTP responses, which tunnels pass through.
- **Fonts and KaTeX are bundled** — no CDN requests at runtime.

## 5. Risks and trade-offs

| Risk | Mitigation |
|---|---|
| The syllabus is a transcription. | Marked in the data; trivially replaceable; nothing else depends on its wording. |
| FIT rules and courses will change before 2029. | Snapshots carry `validFor`, `retrievedOn`, `source`; UI shows age and a re-verify prompt; update procedure documented. |
| Content volume: a year of maths is a lot to author. | Problem *generators* give unlimited variants per skill. Depth first on the topics being taught now; coverage is reported honestly in the roadmap. |
| Authored difficulty and model constants are untested on a real learner. | All derived state is recomputable from the event log, so the model can be retuned without losing history. |
